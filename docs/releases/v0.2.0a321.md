# BreadSched 0.2.0a321

Application version: `0.2.0a321`

Native schema version: `14`

Supported native schemas: `6–14`

This alpha prices the shares a scheduled transaction buys or sells (#417).

## Highlights

- **Shares from a price.** A payroll schedule that puts $500.00 into a 401(k) fund
  used to record 500 shares. Entering it now divides the amount by the fund's
  latest price on or before that date.
- **A quote, or your price.** Before entering, BreadSched fetches an online quote
  for a fund that has a quote source. **Share prices** then shows each fund's
  price and shares to confirm. Where a fund has no price, type its share price or
  the shares from your statement; what you type is saved as that date's price. The
  desktop, the browser, and the command line (`--share-price`, `--shares`, or a
  prompt) all work this way.
- **Nothing entered unpriced.** An automatic schedule whose shares nothing prices
  stays due until you give a price.

## Command-line and browser compatibility

`due-review` and `scheduled-next` gain `--share-price SYMBOL=PRICE`,
`--shares SYMBOL=COUNT`, and `--no-online-quotes`; `scheduled --post` gains
`--no-online-quotes`. A command that would enter shares with no price now stops and
names the fund. `/api/v1` adds `POST /due-review/quotes`, and `POST /due-review`
and `POST /schedules/{handle}/next` accept an optional `prices` list. The browser
adds `/api/due-review/quotes`.

## Native-book compatibility

No schema change. A typed price is an ordinary dated price.

## Upgrade and rollback

Keep an independent backup of a real book. Going back to 0.2.0a320 needs no
restore; shares entered by this version keep their quantities.

## Verified artifacts

After the complete CI matrix succeeds on main, the release workflow publishes a
source distribution, pure-Python wheel, Windows installer, Flatpak bundle, Debian
and Fedora packages, and `SHA256SUMS` for the tested commit.
